# Tech Debt: Buy Cheap, Buy Twice

Technical debt really is what it says on the tin. We all know that you have to pay back a debt eventually. And the longer you wait, the more you pay. Technical debt is that - you can move fast now, but you *will* pay for it one day.

Picture this: your house has a bathroom upstairs, but you need a downstairs toilet. You ask a builder to help you - in this analogy, your eager builder is an AI coding agent. It quickly spots that there’s space under the stairs and there’s existing piping nearby. It’s cramped, but it does the job. Quick, easy, sorted. It’s looked at what it can, used what you’ve told it, and come up with a valid solution.

What it doesn’t have is the experience of an architect who’s designed a hundred houses, or any idea about your long-term plans for the house. It knows what it’s been told: add a downstairs toilet. So that’s what it does.

It doesn’t take long to realise the slanted ceiling is more than an inconvenience. And when you eventually want a proper bathroom downstairs - a shower, room to breathe - you’re not extending what you built. You’re getting planning permission, building a proper extension, *and* tearing out what was under the stairs first. You pay for the quick solution, you pay for the right solution, and you pay to undo the shortcut on top of it.

Going fast is a legitimate choice. Sometimes the speed is genuinely worth the higher price you’ll pay down the line. But you need to know that’s the deal you’re making. The bill always arrives, and it’s always larger than it looked at the time. Speed is never free; it’s just deferred.
