# CoPilot, Claude, et al 

I'm one of the slow curious adopters of AI, beginning life incredibly skeptical and not believing any of the sensationalist stuff about it taking our jobs. 

I think there's some value in talking about the *really early* beginnings of using AI in your day to day. I've found so far that all of the AI talk is very quick to go quite deep into how much you can embed it and lean on it. Too quickly you're reading about MCP servers and plugging agents into agents. 

What about for those that have never used it beyond asking it a simple google-able question?

## Getting started

I've found it was important to get it close to your code, in the IDE. IntelliJ and VS Code have decent enough plug-ins already, and that gives you the useful ability to just drag files from your project structure into the chat-box, to add as context. 

## Prompts

Everyone talks about good prompts. When someone gets something good out of AI, you ask "can you share the prompt you used?". There is no single god-prompt. You should get used to talking to it. Develop ideas - in a chat, tell it what you're trying to do. Feed it context about why you're trying to do it. Ask it to help you forge a plan. You can almost go into waterfall mode - have it help you build a short spec with a lot of specific details - then start a new chat, give it the spec, and ask it to implement it. 

## Get confidence

Not confidence for you in using it. Confidence that it's good. Because it is. Hopefully it's not going to take your job, but it can quickly become a force multiplier for you if you lean into it. 

Ask it questions about your codebase that you know the answers to. Validate what it outputs. You need to get past any cyncism you might have. It's a lot more than Siri on your iPhone. 

Next time you're exploring a new area of code, take it with you. Ask it questions about the inner-workings, get it to summarise the entry point and the capabilities. 

## Tie your hands behind your back

I'm writing this using vim, as I attempt to teach myself to use vim well enough to not use the mouse at all. The technique is to force myself to only use the keyboard. It'll make me learn better. 

Try that with your next PR. Get claude, tell it what you're trying to do, form that spec, give it the context about your codebase, give it example files of similar things elsewhere, and don't write a line of code yourself. You'll learn more in one PR exclusively using Claude than you will in weeks of asking AI random questions.
